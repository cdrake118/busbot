# Goal: Build an AI-Operated Solopreneur Business Portfolio

## Mission

Build and operate a portfolio of small businesses that Cory and Austin can run
as solopreneurs, with AI agents doing the bulk of the day-to-day work —
marketing, content, customer communication, operations, and analytics. The
humans set direction, approve spending, and handle anything requiring legal
signatures or in-person presence; the agents do everything else.

## Financial Targets

- **Year 1:** on track to earn **$100,000** in combined revenue across the
  portfolio (i.e., exiting year 1 at a run rate that supports this).
- **Long term:** grow to **$350,000/year** in combined revenue.
- These do not need to be venture-scale businesses. Durable, cash-flowing,
  low-maintenance businesses beat high-growth/high-burn ones.

## Hard Constraints

1. **Minimal capital investment.** Prefer businesses that can launch for under
   a few hundred dollars (domains, hosting, basic tooling). No inventory-heavy,
   equipment-heavy, or capital-intensive models.
2. **Efficient growth.** Default to organic channels — SEO, content, word of
   mouth, community presence, and product quality. Paid advertising is allowed
   when the return on investment is demonstrably strong: start with small test
   budgets, measure customer acquisition cost against revenue per customer,
   and scale spend only on proven positive returns.
3. **Two-person headcount, forever.** Every business must be operable by two
   people plus AI agents. If a business can only grow by hiring, it doesn't fit.
4. **AI-leverageable.** Favor businesses where the core work (content,
   research, code, support, outreach) is work AI agents are genuinely good at.

## What Success Looks Like

- 2–4 live businesses, each with a clear niche, an automated marketing engine,
  and recurring or repeatable revenue.
- A weekly operating rhythm where AI agents produce the work and the humans
  spend a few hours reviewing, approving, and steering.
- Diversified income: no single business is more than ~60% of total revenue.

## Scope of Work for Claude

1. **Research & selection.** Identify and validate candidate business ideas
   against the constraints above: market demand, competition, realistic
   revenue per customer, and how much of the operation AI can actually run.
   Rank them and recommend a starting set.
2. **Launch.** For each selected business: name, positioning, landing
   page/site, pricing, and a launch checklist. Build any software, automation,
   or content systems needed, in this repo.
3. **Marketing engine.** Design and run the organic growth system per
   business: SEO/content pipelines, social presence, email, community
   engagement — automated wherever possible, with human review gates on
   anything published under our names. Where paid ads make sense, propose
   small ROI tests with clear success metrics before any scaled spend.
4. **Operations.** Build the agent workflows that handle fulfillment, customer
   communication, and reporting. Track revenue and costs per business against
   the $100k year-1 target and flag anything off track.
5. **Iterate or kill.** Review each business's traction honestly. Recommend
   doubling down, fixing, or shutting down based on data, not sunk cost.

## Guardrails

- **No spending money, signing up for paid services, or entering agreements
  without explicit human approval.** Propose; don't purchase.
- **Human approval before anything is published externally** (posts, emails,
  listings, launches) until a business's review process is explicitly relaxed.
- **Stay legal and reputable.** No spam, scraped-content farms, fake reviews,
  impersonation, or gray-hat SEO. These businesses carry our names.
- **Be honest about numbers.** Report real traction, including bad news, and
  state assumptions behind any revenue projections.

## Operating Notes

- Treat this repository (`busbot`) as the home base: business plans, agent
  workflows, code, content systems, and revenue tracking all live here.
- Default cadence: propose → human approves → agent executes → report results.
- When two paths are viable, pick the one that is cheaper to test and faster
  to get real customer feedback from.
