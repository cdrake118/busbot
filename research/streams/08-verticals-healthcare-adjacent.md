# Vertical Stream 8: Healthcare-Adjacent Practices Evaluation (2024–2026)

## Method and confidence key
18 web searches, 2024–2026 sources prioritized. Confidence flags: **[H]** = corroborated by association/government/academic data; **[M]** = single trade source or consistent vendor-side data; **[L]** = vendor marketing claims, directional only. Recurring hazard: most "no-show rate" and "missed call" statistics circulating online are published by AI-receptionist vendors themselves — flagged throughout.

## Vertical-by-vertical (9 verticals assessed)

### 1. Dental — large, rich, and the most saturated AI category in SMB healthcare
- **Economics:** ~135k–178k practice establishments ([ADA via DentistEmailList](https://www.dentistemaillist.com/blog/how-many-dental-practices-in-the-us)) [M]; general practices produce ~$65k–$120k/month ([Overjet](https://www.overjet.com/blog/average-dental-practice-revenue-in-2025-complete-breakdown-by-specialty)) [M]. Ownership fell 84.7% (2005) → ~73% (2021); DSO affiliation 27% among recent grads ([ADA HPI](https://www.ada.org/-/media/project/ada-organization/ada/ada-org/files/resources/research/hpi/practice_ownership_trends_dentistry_new_look_old_data.pdf)) [H].
- **Pain:** Contested. Vendor blogs claim 15–20% no-shows ([Clerri](https://clerri.com/blog/dental-patient-no-show-statistics)) [L], but ADA-adjacent data shows no-shows dropped to ~4% in 2023 ([Becker's Dental](https://www.beckersdental.com/dentists/where-dental-practices-stand-on-scheduling-patient-retention/)) [M]. The "front desk misses 35% of calls" figure is Arini's own marketing [L].
- **SATURATION — confirmed gold rush.** **15+ platforms actively market dental AI receptionists as of early 2026** ([Orthia roundup](https://orthia.io/blog/dental-ai-receptionist-competition)) [M]: Arini (YC W24), Viva AI, Dentina, Rondah, Peerlogic, Attainment, Savvy Agents, OrthoBerry. Incumbents bundling: Weave acquired TrueLark May 2025 ([Weave/TrueLark](https://www.getweave.com/truelark/)); Adit shipped AI Front Desk to 5,000+ practices (Feb 2026). Prices compressed to $200–$500/mo. Practitioner backlash exists: ["AI Dental Receptionists – The Latest Grift"](https://www.dentaltown.com/blog/post/22270/ai-dental-receptionists-the-latest-grift) on Dentaltown [M for sentiment]. Incumbent contract abuse documented (Solutionreach cancellation windows, Podium data lock-in — [Emitrr](https://emitrr.com/blog/solutionreach-vs-weave/), [Quo](https://www.quo.com/blog/weave-alternatives/)) [M].
- **HIPAA:** Full covered-entity territory; BAAs required.
- **WTP:** Excellent — marketing retainers $2.5k–$4.5k/mo ([PMAX](https://www.pmaxdentalmarketing.com/how-much-do-dental-marketing-agencies-charge/)) [M].
- **Verdict: rich buyers, but the worst saturation in the cluster on both service and media sides.**

### 2. Veterinary — dental's pain profile, minus HIPAA, with one-third the vendor density
- **Economics:** ~32,000–34,000 US practices; corporate share ~25–36% of GPs (75% of specialty/ER), so **~20,000+ independent GP clinics remain** ([AAHA](https://www.aaha.org/trends-magazine/publications/corporate-consolidation-and-the-rise-of-private-equity/), [Mansfield Advisors](https://www.mansfieldadvisors.com/blog/us-veterinary-consolidation-a-land-of-opportunity/)) [H]. Revenue per patient ~$622/yr; visits declined 2.3% in 2024 — clinics actively hunting for client retention ([Today's Veterinary Business/Vetsource](https://todaysveterinarybusiness.com/client-visits-vetsource-010525/), [AVMA](https://www.avma.org/news/less-foot-traffic-veterinary-practices-spells-declining-revenue)) [H]. Consolidator distress rising ([Octus on VSO credit pressure](https://octus.com/resources/articles/private-credit-exposure-to-veterinary-rollups-shows-growing-dispersion-vsos-under-increasing-pressure/)); academics argue for a "resurgent independent segment" ([Frontiers SWOT, 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/veterinary-science/articles/10.3389/fvets.2025.1558745/full)) [M].
- **Pain:** Vendor-claimed 30–40% missed-call rates ([Dialzara](https://dialzara.com/industries/veterinary-clinics)) [L — no independent phone-abandonment studies found; diligence gap]. Declining visit frequency = documented recall/reactivation gap [H].
- **Saturation:** Players exist — Dodo (YC, seed-scale, "hundreds of clinics" — [YC](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/dodo), [PitchBook](https://pitchbook.com/profiles/company/634616-11)), Dialzara, Smith.ai — but **funding is seed-scale, no Weave-equivalent bundling event, nothing like dental's 15-vendor density** [M].
- **Compliance: NO HIPAA.** Animal records aren't PHI; only state confidentiality rules apply. The least-regulated clinical-feeling vertical — decisive for a 2-person shop [H].
- **WTP:** Clinics already pay human after-hours answering and relief staffing; $1M+ typical GP revenue supports $1k–$2.5k retainers [M].
- **Reachability: excellent.** VHMA (4,500+ managers, sells sponsorships, 91% decision-makers — [VHMA](https://www.vhma.org/resources/sponsor-opportunities24222)), dvm360 (60,000+ professionals), Uncharted Veterinary, and a new association of independent veterinarians formed explicitly to "combat corporate" ([dvm360](https://www.dvm360.com/view/combating-corporate-new-association-unites-independent-veterinarians)) — identity-driven independent-operator audience with no dominant indie newsletter [M].

### 3. Med spas — fastest growth, cash-pay, documented automation gap, weakest incumbents
- **Economics:** ~10,500 spas (2023) → ~11,500+ (2025); **average revenue $1.4M/spa, ~15%/yr growth** ([AmSpa State of the Industry](https://americanmedspa.org/resources/med-spa-statistics)) [H]. Overwhelmingly independent owner-operators [M].
- **Pain — best-documented lead-leak in the cluster:** only **18% of med spas use marketing automation**; 50% of leads book with a competitor before the spa responds; rebooking 40–50% vs ~90% in dentistry ([Dewy CRM](https://www.dewy.io/blog/lead-conversion-best-practices-for-medical-aesthetic-practices), [Wellness MD Group](https://wellnessmdgroup.com/blog/med-spa-lead-generation-2026), [AmSpa](https://www.americanmedspa.org/news/marketing-metrics-that-matter-how-to-improve-lead-conversion-and-roi/)) [M — vendor-adjacent but consistent].
- **Saturation: low-moderate.** Zenoti bundles an AI receptionist (~$400/mo, tied to PMS switch — [Zenoti](https://www.zenoti.com/ai-workforce/ai-receptionist)); no standalone med-spa AI-ops gold rush [M].
- **HIPAA — adversarial correction:** "cash-pay so no HIPAA" is wrong in spirit. Med spas with licensed providers storing ePHI need HIPAA-grade handling; AmSpa pushes compliance ([AmSpa HIPAA FAQ](https://americanmedspa.org/blog/hipaa-faqs-navigating-hipaa-compliance-in-your-medical-spa)) [H]. Practical friction still lower than insurance-billing verticals: marketing-funnel work is pre-patient/PHI-light.
- **WTP: highest relative to ticket size.** $2.5k–$5k+/mo marketing retainers habitual; 4–7% of revenue on marketing normative [M].
- **Reachability:** AmSpa: 18,000 email subscribers, 4,000+ paid members, 83% decision-makers, active vendor-sponsorship machine ([AmSpa](https://www.americanmedspa.org/work-with-us/)) [H]. Gap: no operator-benchmarks newsletter.

### 4. Physical therapy — severe pain, but EMRs are eating the AI layer and payers eat the margin
~37,000 clinics, avg revenue ~$871k; heavy PE roll-up (4 PE deals 2010 → 175 in 2023 — [Stifel](https://www.stifel.com/Newsletters/InvestmentBanking/BAL/Marketing/Healthcare/PhysicalTherapy/2025/PT_MarketOverview072025.pdf)) [H]. Pain spectacular — ~70% of patients don't complete plans of care ([ScienceDirect 2025](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S2468781225000748)) [H] — but reimbursement is ~$80–$110/visit and the AI layer ships *inside* the EMRs (Prompt, SPRY, WebPT) ([Prompt](https://www.prompthealth.com/ai-automation)) [M]. Full HIPAA + eligibility/auth burden. **Weak fit.**

### 5. Chiropractic — independent and unconsolidated, but small wallets
~39,000 offices, **average gross only $385k** ([Dynamic Chiropractic](https://dynamicchiropractic.com/article/102463-private-equity-and-the-future-of-chiropractic-pt-1)) [M]; PE largely absent; almost no dedicated AI vendors — genuine white space — but a hard sell at $1.5k+/mo; coaching ecosystem skews MLM-ish. **White space without wallet; viable only at $750–$1,200/mo.**

### 6. Mental health / therapy group practices — huge admin pain, maximal compliance sensitivity
~Half of provider hours go to non-clinical admin ([mdhub](https://www.mdhub.ai/blog-posts/mental-health-practice-management-tools-2026)) [M]; but AI vendors proliferate on SimplePractice/TherapyNotes rails; PHI is the most sensitive class; thin margins; reputational risk of AI mishandling a suicidal caller is existential for a 2-person vendor. **Avoid as primary.**

### 7. Home care agencies — real ops chaos, wrong shape of problem
20,000+ private-duty agencies, $1M–$3M median revenue, 77% caregiver turnover ([NCH Stats](https://nchstats.com/us-home-care-industry-stats/)) [H]. Core pain is *caregiver shift-fill and churn*, not front-desk/recall — and scheduling platforms (CareSmartz360, Careswitch, Phoebe) race to automate exactly that [M]. Media owned: Home Health Care News + HCAOA. **Service package doesn't map to #1 pain; newsletter lane occupied.**

### 8. Fertility/IVF — too few, too consolidated
~500 US clinics; PE-affiliated clinics perform ~30% of cycles ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/art/success-rates/index.html), [RBEJ 2025](https://rbej.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s12958-025-01446-4)) [H]. TAM of independent buyers is a few hundred. **Eliminate.**

### 9. Urgent care — corporate category
14,423 centers, 82% multi-unit, ~45% corporate chains, 18% PE-backed ([UCA](https://urgentcareassociation.org/center-counts/), [JUCM](https://www.jucm.com/private-equity-investment-in-urgent-care-by-number-of-centers-2025/)) [H]. Independent owner-operator buyer barely exists. **Eliminate.**

### Optometry (partial)
Thin vendor landscape, good recall economics (eyewear reorder cycles), independents persist — but weaker community/pain documentation in this pass [L — under-researched; candidate for follow-up].

## Adversarial findings on the two framing questions

**Is dental AI saturated?** Yes. 15+ funded vendors, an acquisition wave (Weave/TrueLark), platform bundling, price compression to $200–$500/mo, and visible practitioner backlash. The surviving dental wedge is a managed-service position — defensible, but the noisiest channel in SMB healthcare.

**Does HIPAA kill the 2-person model?** No, but it taxes it. BAAs are obtainable from every major LLM/API provider ([OpenAI Help](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/8660679-how-can-i-get-a-business-associate-agreement-baa-with-openai)); the real burden is being *audit-ready as a business associate* — the proposed 2026 Security Rule update makes safeguards mandatory with annual audits for business associates ([Medcurity](https://medcurity.com/hipaa-business-associate-agreement-template/)) [M]. Budget ~$5–15k/yr and process discipline for clinical verticals. That tax is avoidable in exactly one vertical: **veterinary**.

## TOP 2 PICKS

### Pick 1: Veterinary (independent general practices)
The only vertical combining (a) 20,000+ independent owner-operators with $1M+ revenue, (b) documented demand decline making recall/reactivation a board-level worry, (c) seed-stage-only AI competition with no incumbent bundling event, (d) **zero HIPAA**, and (e) a reachable, identity-charged community (VHMA, dvm360, Uncharted, the new independent-vet association) with proven vendor sponsorship spend.

- **Service package ($1,500–$2,500/mo):** "The Independent Clinic Ops Desk" — AI phone overflow/after-hours answering with emergency-triage escalation rules, lapsed-patient reactivation campaigns (the 2.3% visit decline is the sales deck), vaccine/dental-cleaning recall sequences, review generation, pet-insurance claim paperwork assistance. Position against Mars/NVA: "corporate-grade ops without selling to corporate." Sell "AI + your rules," never autonomous triage.
- **Newsletter:** independent-practice-operator brief (benchmarks, consolidator deal tracking, "what NVA pays for a clinic like yours," staffing and pricing data). The anti-corporate identity is an editorial wedge no incumbent owns. Sponsors: PMS vendors, diagnostics (IDEXX/Zoetis ecosystem), relief-staffing platforms, practice lenders/brokers.

### Pick 2: Med spas / aesthetics
Highest growth (15%/yr), $1.4M average revenue, cash-pay high-ticket, **best-documented automation gap in the cluster** (18% automation adoption, 40–50% rebooking vs dentistry's 90%), habitual $2.5k–$5k/mo agency retainers, only platform-bundled AI competition. HIPAA applies more than owners think — a *selling point* for a vendor who handles it properly; workload is mostly pre-patient and PHI-light.

- **Service package ($1,500–$2,500/mo):** "Revenue Recovery for Med Spas" — 5-minute speed-to-lead follow-up on every form/DM/missed call, consult no-show prevention, post-visit rebooking tied to treatment cadence (tox every 90 days), membership churn saves, review velocity. ROI anchor: one recovered Botox patient/month covers the retainer. Operate HIPAA-clean and market that discipline.
- **Newsletter:** the operator/benchmark gap next to AmSpa — "med spa unit economics weekly" (injector comp data, device ROI teardowns, GLP-1 revenue analysis, compliance enforcement news, PE deal terms). Device makers, injectable suppliers, financing companies are proven sponsors.

**Runner-up / hedge:** chiropractic + optometry as low-compliance expansion verticals once the playbook is productized; avoid PT, mental health, fertility, urgent care.

**Key diligence gaps before committing:** (1) independent verification of vet missed-call rates (all current figures vendor-published — run a secret-shopper call study on 50 clinics); (2) Dodo's actual traction/funding (sources conflict); (3) whether the contested dental no-show pattern (4% ADA vs 15–20% vendor) repeats in vet — if true no-show pain is low, lead capture and reactivation should headline the pitch, not no-show prevention.
