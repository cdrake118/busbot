# Business Candidates: Ranked Shortlist & Recommended Starting Set

*Synthesized from five parallel deep-research streams (June 2026), covering
productized AI services, micro-SaaS, content/newsletter businesses,
directories/lead-gen/API products, and a cross-cutting reality check on what
AI agents can actually operate autonomously. ~80 sources, 2024–2026 evidence,
with adversarial verification — every stream was instructed to hunt for
failure rates and course-seller hype, not just survivor stories. Full stream
summaries with per-claim citations are in `research/streams/`.*

---

## Part 1: What the evidence actually says (cross-cutting findings)

These five findings repeat across independent research streams and should
govern every decision below.

### 1. Median outcomes are near zero in every model; services are the exception

- Median Stripe-verified indie product: **$169/month**; ~54% make $0
  (5,079-product analysis, 2025). Median time to $1k MRR: **12–18 months**.
- Median earning Substack writer: ~$4,000/year. Median Gumroad product: $364
  *lifetime*. Median Chrome extension: 17 installs. ~70% of Skool communities
  earn $0.
- Stripe's own 2025 data: median first-six-month solo-founder revenue **fell
  23% YoY** while the top decile rose 19% — more entrants, shrinking median,
  winners take more (top-decile-to-median gap: 61x).
- The one model with a documented, repeatable year-one $100k path is
  **productized services**: 5–10 SMB clients at $1,000–$2,500/month retainers,
  4–8 weeks to first client. This matches verified pricing benchmarks and
  doesn't require believing any guru's numbers.

### 2. Every loud success story is monetized by its teller

Nick Saraev ($72k/month automation agency → makes more selling the course),
Liam Ottley ($18M claims, sells $5–7k accelerators, 280k students flooding the
niche), John Rush (directory guru, 25% hit rate by his own numbers, sells the
guide), Skool leaderboards (affiliate-driven). **Rule adopted: never enter a
niche at the point where its promoter sends their students.** Generic "AI
automation agency," generic chatbots, cold-outreach automation, Notion
templates, and Etsy digital products are all post-peak and flooded.

### 3. "AI runs the business while I sleep" has no verified case

- Anthropic's own Project Vend: an unsupervised agent running a real shop lost
  money to social engineering and irrational discounting. Gartner forecasts
  >40% of agentic-AI projects canceled by 2027.
- Klarna publicly reversed its AI-support-replaces-700-agents claim and rehired
  humans after quality dropped.
- What IS verified: AI handles **60–70% of tier-1 support tickets**
  (Intercom Fin: 67% resolution over 40M conversations), **70–80% of
  bookkeeping categorization**, **60–80% of content drafting and code**, and
  **30–50% of outbound (research/drafting only)**. Closing sales, pricing,
  strategy, and judgment remain ~0% automatable.
- Realistic human load: **10–20 hrs/week per stabilized business** after a
  3–6 month build phase at 20–40 hrs/week. With two operators, that caps us at
  2–3 simultaneous businesses — which matches the goal.

### 4. Sequence, don't parallelize

Every dataset says splitting attention before first traction is the dominant
failure mode. The famous "portfolio" solopreneurs are misleading: Pieter
Levels gets ~70% of revenue from 1 of 40+ launches; John Rush's directories
exist partly to market his SaaS tools. The portfolio is a **search process for
winners**, not diversification. Corollary: a second *channel* on business #1
is worth more than a second business — the 2026 platform risk map (AI
Overviews eating 50%+ of clicks, Google's Nov 2025 hard email rejection rules,
X API at $0.20/post-with-URL, YouTube/LinkedIn authenticity policies) can kill
any single-channel business overnight.

### 5. Verticalization is the only durable moat for a tiny team

AI commoditized horizontal everything (wrappers die on a 2–3 year absorption
cycle; agencies face "AI discount" pressure; translation rates halved). What
survived 2024–2026 with pricing power: **vertical depth** — legal AI tools at
$500–2,000/month/customer, healthcare intake at $600–1,500/month, workflow
embeddedness in "boring" industries, compliance-adjacent niches, and trust
assets (audience, reviews, integrations) that clones can't copy.

## Part 2: Ranked shortlist (specific plays, not categories)

Scored against: time-to-revenue, year-one revenue potential, AI-operability,
moat durability, startup cost, and evidence quality.

### #1 — Vertical productized AI-operations service for one SMB niche
**The cash engine. Start immediately.**

Pick ONE vertical (selection criteria in Part 4) and sell a fixed-scope,
fixed-price monthly service: e.g., AI-powered intake + follow-up + review
management + back-office automation for that industry, delivered as a
productized package ($1,000–$2,500/month + $2–5k setup), NOT hourly
consulting.

- **Evidence:** SMB AI investment hit 57% in 2025 (PayPal survey); Upwork AI
  skill demand +109% YoY; MIT data shows externally-implemented AI succeeds
  ~2x as often as internal builds; agency retainer benchmarks verified at
  $1.5–5k/month. Credible analogs: My AskAI (2 people, ~$500k ARR), Designjoy
  (1 person, $1M+/yr — took 5 years).
- **Math:** 6 clients × $1,500/month + setup fees ≈ $100k/year run rate.
- **Time to first revenue:** 4–8 weeks.
- **AI fraction:** 60–80% of delivery (builds, drafts, monitoring); humans do
  discovery, sales calls, QA, escalations.
- **Failure modes:** churn (~20–30% of clients within months), scope creep,
  "AI discount" pricing pressure. Mitigate by pricing on business outcome
  (leads handled, hours saved), not on AI labor.

### #2 — Niche B2B newsletter in the same vertical
**The compounding asset. Start in parallel (it shares all research with #1).**

Sponsorship-first (77% of successful new publications monetize via ads, not
paywalls), paid tier later.

- **Evidence:** verified B2B CPMs of $50–150 ($80–200 for hard-to-reach
  audiences); an 8,000-subscriber niche B2B list commands ~$2,000/placement;
  median time-to-first-dollar fell to 66 days (Beehiiv 2026); honest
  free-to-paid conversion is 2–5%, not the platform-marketed 10%.
- **Math:** 5,000 engaged subs by month 9–10 × $50–100 CPM × 8 sends/month ≈
  $2–4k/month by Q4, plus it generates inbound leads for #1.
- **Time to meaningful revenue:** 6–9 months. Months 1–4 ≈ $0.
- **AI fraction:** high — research synthesis, curation drafts, repurposing;
  humans own voice, judgment, and sponsor relationships.
- **Failure modes:** generic AI-niche newsletters are saturated; must be
  vertical + operator-focused. Growth without an audience requires Boosts/
  recommendations/paid acquisition (~$2–4 per subscriber, ROI-positive against
  B2B CPMs — this is where the ad budget goes).

### #3 — Vertical B2B workflow micro-SaaS, productized from #1's delivery
**The equity asset. Start month 5–7, only after the service reveals the
repeated pain point.**

Whatever we build 3+ times for service clients becomes the product. Vertical
workflow tools are the only micro-SaaS category where tiny teams kept pricing
power ($300–2,000/month/customer) and exit multiples (3.9x profit on
Acquire.com; record PE appetite for vertical SaaS).

- **Math:** $4k MRR by month 12 is a realistic stretch (≈ $25k year-one
  cumulative) given we'd start with paying service clients as design partners
  — this sidesteps the cold-start problem that kills 70% of micro-SaaS under
  $1k MRR.
- **Failure modes:** building before validation; vibe-coded security holes
  (documented breaches — every AI-built app needs a security pass);
  horizontal drift.

### #4 — Shopify app (alternative or successor to #3)
Best risk-adjusted marketplace: built-in buyer intent, developers keep 100% of
the first $1M lifetime revenue, lowest flood rate of any marketplace (~257
apps/month added vs. thousands of AI tools). Boring merchant-ops problems pay
real monthly fees. Choose this over #3 if the service vertical doesn't yield a
clear SaaS wedge.

### #5 — Lead-gen micro-directories in high-ticket local niches
**Optional side bets, 80%+ AI-built. Only website-based lead-gen — never
Google Business Profile games (active federal litigation), never
display-ad-monetized (AI Overviews ate that traffic).**

- **Evidence:** documented wins are $1–5k/month per site after 6–12 months
  with ~1-in-3-or-4 hit rate (the model's own promoter's numbers). Leads in
  legal/home-services verticals benchmark at $200–320 CPL ad-equivalent.
- Treat as a portfolio experiment: 2–3 attempts, ~$50/site cost, kill fast.

### #6 — Digital products attached to the newsletter audience
Templates/tools/mini-courses at $50–200 launched to the list, from month 8+.
Every documented template success was downstream of an audience. Realistic:
$10–25k/year, lumpy. Never a standalone play (median Gumroad product: $364
lifetime).

### Explicitly avoided (verified negative evidence)
| Play | Why |
|---|---|
| New SEO content/affiliate sites | 60%+ zero-click, AIO 25%+ referral drop, <25% HCU recovery |
| Generic "AI automation agency" | 280k course students flooding it; commoditized |
| Thin GPT wrappers | 2–3 year model-absorption cycle, documented die-off |
| GBP rank-and-rent | Google federal lawsuit + 80% suspension surge |
| Translation/localization | Verified 60–80% practitioner income collapse |
| Etsy/Notion templates as primary | Post-peak, AI-flooded, audience-dependent |
| Chrome extensions as primary | Median 17 installs |
| API marketplaces | RapidAPI collapsed; sold to Nokia 2024 |
| Cold-email-at-scale motions | Google/Microsoft hard enforcement; 3.4% reply rates |
| Paid community / job board (cold-start) | Median $0 without pre-existing audience |

## Part 3: Recommended starting set & sequencing

**Phase 1 (months 0–2): ONE vertical, TWO motions.**
1. **Vertical productized AI service** (#1) — the cash engine. Target: first
   client by week 6–8.
2. **Vertical B2B newsletter** (#2) — the compounding asset. Costs almost
   nothing extra because it's the same research, and it becomes the service's
   lead source.

These are deliberately NOT two businesses — they're one vertical bet with a
fast-cash motion and a slow-compounding motion. This respects the strongest
finding in the research (sequence, don't parallelize) while still staggering
time-to-revenue.

**Phase 2 (months 5–7, gated):** when the service is at 4+ clients and <15
hrs/week of human time with playbooks written, productize the most-repeated
build into the **vertical micro-SaaS** (#3) or a **Shopify app** (#4), selling
first to existing clients and the newsletter list.

**Side bets (anytime, capped at ~5% of effort):** 2–3 lead-gen directories
(#5) as cheap AI-built experiments. **Month 8+:** digital products (#6) to the
newsletter list.

**Kill criteria:** service — no paying client in 10 weeks → change vertical,
not model. Newsletter — <1,500 subs or <40% open rate at month 6 → reposition.
SaaS — <5 paying customers within 90 days of launch → fold features back into
the service. Directories — no revenue in 6 months → kill.

## Part 4: Vertical selection criteria (next decision)

The single highest-leverage decision is the vertical. Filters from the
evidence:
1. **High revenue per customer event** ($500+ jobs/clients) so $1–2.5k/month
   retainers and $50–200 leads are obviously worth it.
2. **Boring and under-served** — trades, specialty clinics, niche professional
   services; not where course-sellers send students (e-commerce brands,
   coaches, agencies).
3. **Visible AI-operable pain**: intake/scheduling, quote follow-up, review
   management, document/back-office processing.
4. **An operator audience reachable for the newsletter** (association lists,
   Facebook/LinkedIn groups, trade pubs that take sponsorships).
5. **Ideally adjacent to something Cory or Austin already knows** — domain
   credibility is the unfakeable part.

## Part 5: Year-one projection (stated assumptions)

Assumptions: ~25–35 combined human hours/week; AI agents at the verified
automation fractions (not the fantasy ones); first service client by month 2;
~25% service churn; $3–6k total ad/growth spend deployed only against measured
CAC (mostly newsletter acquisition at $2–4/subscriber); no pre-existing
audience.

| Stream | Conservative | Base | Stretch |
|---|---|---|---|
| Productized service (from month 2) | $45k | $65k | $90k |
| Newsletter (sponsors, from month 6–8) | $5k | $12k | $25k |
| Micro-SaaS / Shopify app (from month 8+) | $2k | $8k | $20k |
| Directories + digital products | $1k | $5k | $12k |
| **Year-one total** | **$53k** | **$90k** | **$147k** |

**Honest framing:** $100k year-one cash collected is the base-to-stretch
band, and it is carried almost entirely by the service in months 1–9 — the
research found essentially no documented cases of $100k year-one from new
*products* without a pre-existing audience. Exiting year one at a
**$120–180k run rate** is the realistic success condition, which puts the
$350k/year goal on a 3-year trajectory: year two scales SaaS MRR and
newsletter CPM revenue (the compounding assets) while the service is
deliberately capped (~$15–18k/month max for two humans) and increasingly
automated.
