# Vertical Selection: Ranked Candidates & Recommendation

*Synthesized from four parallel research streams (June 2026) evaluating ~35
verticals across home services/trades, professional services, healthcare-
adjacent practices, and overlooked B2B niches — each scored against the
filters in `business-candidates.md` Part 4: revenue per customer event,
under-served/boring, AI-operable pain, reachable operator audience, and
competitive saturation. Full cluster reports with per-claim citations:
`streams/06–09`.*

---

## The decisive cross-cutting finding

**"AI answers your missed calls" is no longer white space anywhere big.**
Avoca raised $125M+ at a $1B valuation targeting HVAC/plumbing/roofing;
Jobber bundles an AI receptionist at $99/month; Weave acquired TrueLark and
sells dental AI at $200–500/month; 15+ funded vendors fight over dental
alone. Any vertical where the funded players already operate — or where the
incumbent CRM is bundling AI — is disqualified for the service. The retainer
must be won on what software-in-a-box can't do: managed multi-step
operations (follow-up cadences, document automation, reactivation campaigns,
review ops) for verticals the funded players ignore.

~25 verticals were disqualified outright, including: HVAC, plumbing, roofing,
pest control (saturated + PE roll-ups), dental (worst saturation in SMB
healthcare), personal-injury law and real estate agents (most over-served of
all), accounting (newsletter lane owned by incumbents), auto repair,
self-storage, freight, staffing, gyms, childcare, moving (CRM-bundled AI),
funeral homes and towing (price-anchored under $500/month), PT and mental
health (EMR-bundled AI + compliance), fertility and urgent care (no
independent buyers left), laundromats (peak boring-business hype, no ops
surface).

## Recommendation: Independent insurance agencies

Restoration has the higher willingness-to-pay and the dual-stream
convergence, but the ranking weighs the constraint that matters most for
this portfolio: **the business must be operable by two people plus AI agents
at 10–20 hrs/week.** On that test, insurance wins decisively:

1. **The work shape is maximally AI-operable.** COI processing, renewal
   sequences, cross-sell campaigns, and document chasing are asynchronous,
   business-hours, document-centric tasks — exactly the 70–80% automation
   band from the operability research. Restoration's core offer is 24/7
   emergency intake where a missed water loss costs the client thousands;
   that's an on-call burden and a liability profile a 2-person team should
   not anchor its first business on.
2. **The sales motion is substitution, not evangelism.** Agencies already
   budget $1,400–2,600/month for VAs doing this exact work. "Same output,
   more consistent, slightly cheaper" beats convincing someone to buy a new
   category.
3. **The buyer is the most durable found**: recurring commission income, no
   seasonality, no storm dependence, no PE-consolidation wave at the small
   end, no HIPAA.
4. **The newsletter economics are the strongest**: insurance/finance B2B
   commands the top CPM band ($60–150+), there is no indie operator
   newsletter, and insurtech vendors are proven sponsors.
5. **Defensible against bundling.** The small-agency AMS incumbents
   (HawkSoft, EZLynx) move slowly; the funded AI flows to enterprise
   (Applied Epic). The 24-month bundling risk that hangs over restoration
   (LEVLR et al. adding services) and vet (PMS vendors) is lowest here.

**Restoration is the designated second vertical** — highest WTP, validated
twice over — to be entered only per the Phase 2 gate in
`business-candidates.md` (first business at <15 hrs/week with playbooks),
or chosen first if Cory or Austin has trades/insurance-claims domain
background. **Veterinary is the hedge** if insurance diligence fails.

This recommendation is overridable by one fact the research can't supply:
domain adjacency. If either operator has real background in any finalist's
industry, that vertical moves to #1.

## Validation sprint before full commitment (1–2 weeks, ~$0)

1. **Secret-shopper study**: contact 30–50 small independent agencies as a
   prospective customer; measure response time, follow-up persistence, COI
   turnaround. This replicates the Clio-style study that validated legal
   intake pain — and its results become the newsletter's first
   data-driven lead magnet.
2. **Five operator conversations**: recruit via state Big "I" chapters and
   agency-owner Facebook groups; validate the VA line item, the COI/renewal
   pain ranking, and price tolerance.
3. **AMS feasibility check**: confirm API/integration access for HawkSoft,
   EZLynx, and NowCerts at the small-agency tier (the delivery stack
   depends on it).
4. **Kill criteria**: if agencies show <$1k/month willingness to pay, or
   AMS access is closed to third parties, drop to vertical #2 (restoration)
   and re-run this sprint.
